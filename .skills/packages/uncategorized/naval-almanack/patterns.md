# Patterns — The Almanack of Naval Ravikant

## The Wealth Formula
**When to use**: Auditing any career or wealth-building decision.
**How**: Specific Knowledge + Accountability + Leverage, applied over decades with compound interest. Specific knowledge = untrainable, playful, authentic skills. Accountability = take risk under your own name. Leverage = labor, capital, or code/media (prefer permissionless).
**Trade-offs**: Takes decades; 99% of effort is wasted finding the right 1% to go all-in on.

## Productize Yourself
**When to use**: As the two-question mnemonic for any venture.
**How**: Ask "Is this authentic to me?" (yourself, uniqueness, specific knowledge) and "Am I scaling it?" (productize: labor, capital, code, or media).
**Trade-offs**: Hard part is the decade of figuring out what you can uniquely provide.

## The Leverage Ladder
**When to use**: Choosing a position in an industry.
**How**: Climb from wage labor (0 leverage) → contractor (labor) → developer (capital + accountability) → fund manager (capital at scale) → tech-enabled company (code + capital + specific knowledge). Each level adds leverage, accountability, and specific knowledge.
**Trade-offs**: Higher rungs require more risk, more skill, more accountability — but capture all the upside.

## The 4-Kind Luck Engine
**When to use**: When outcomes feel luck-dependent.
**How**: Skip kinds 1–2 (blind luck, hustle). Cultivate kind 3 (become skilled enough to spot lucky breaks) and kind 4 (build reputation/character so opportunities seek you out — the deep-sea diver who gets offered the treasure).
**Trade-offs**: Deterministic but slow; requires being visibly the best at something.

## The Decision Protocol (If You Can't Decide → No)
**When to use**: Any major life decision (marry, job, house, city, partner).
**How**: If you can't decide with high certainty, the answer is no — options are abundant and long-lived decisions are costly to reverse. If evenly split, run uphill: take the path with more short-term pain.
**Trade-offs**: Rejects good-but-uncertain opportunities; protects against ruin and regret.

## The Reality Filter
**When to use**: Any decision with emotional stakes.
**How**: Shrink the ego, drop desires about the outcome, acknowledge problems publicly, and treat suffering as delayed reality. "What we wish to be true clouds our perception of what is true."
**Trade-offs**: Uncomfortable; forces confronting truths others avoid — which is where progress lives.

## The 3-Options Rule
**When to use**: Any situation that bothers you.
**How**: Choose change it, accept it, or leave it. Never sit wishing you'd change without changing, or leave without leaving. Keep at most one big desire at a time.
**Trade-offs**: Acceptance is the hardest; one desire limits ambition but preserves peace.

## The Positive-Interpretation Reflex
**When to use**: Every minor annoyance or negative judgment.
**How**: Catch the instant negative reaction and ask "What is the positive of this situation?" (late for a meeting → relax and watch the birds). Train until sub-second.
**Trade-offs**: Costs attention at first; risks self-deception if it prevents genuine change.

## The 60-Day Meditation Protocol
**When to use**: Building the mental-fitness habit.
**How**: One hour, first thing in the morning, 60 days: sit with eyes closed and do nothing — don't fight thoughts, don't embrace them. Resolve the "inbox" of unresolved issues toward inbox zero.
**Trade-offs**: One hour/day is a heavy time cost; benefits (peace, awareness) accrue slowly and nonlinearly.

## The Habit Change Loop
**When to use**: Any behavior change.
**How**: Pick one thing → cultivate a desire → visualize it → plan a sustainable path → identify needs, triggers, substitutes → tell your friends → track meticulously. Self-discipline is a bridge to a new self-image; bake in the new self-image.
**Trade-offs**: One habit at a time; ten-year horizon; "try" is a cop-out — commit externally or set a smaller honest goal.

## The Hourly Rate Rule
**When to use**: Any time-allocation decision.
**How**: Set an aspirational hourly rate that feels absurdly high; if it doesn't feel absurd, raise it. Ignore or outsource anything below the rate. Factor time into every decision.
**Trade-offs**: Feels extravagant early; requires discipline to actually believe your rate.

## The Envy Cure (100% Swap Test)
**When to use**: When jealousy appears.
**How**: Ask if you'd do a wholesale 24/7, 100% swap with that person — their reactions, desires, family, happiness level, self-image. If not, there's no point in being jealous.
**Trade-offs**: None — pure subtraction of a poisonous emotion.
