# Chapter 1: Building Wealth

## Core Idea
Making money is not a thing you do — it's a skill you learn. Getting rich is about knowing what to do, who to do it with, and when to do it; it comes from owning assets that earn while you sleep, not from renting out your time.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Wealth ≠ Money ≠ Status**: Wealth is assets that earn while you sleep. Money is social credits — an IOU from society for past value. Status is your place in the social hierarchy.
  - When to use: When deciding what to optimize for in a career or deal.
  - How: Seek wealth, not money or status. Ignore status-game players; they attack wealth creators.

- **Productize Yourself**: The formula for wealth. "Yourself" = uniqueness + accountability + specific knowledge. "Productize" = leverage + scaling.
  - When to use: As the mnemonic test for any wealth-building activity — "Is this authentic to me, and am I scaling it (with labor, capital, code, or media)?"
  - How: Ask both questions; if either answer is no, the path won't compound.

- **Specific Knowledge**: Knowledge you cannot be trained for — if society can train you to do it, society can train someone else to replace you.
  - When to use: Choosing what to work on.
  - How: Find what you did as a kid/teenager almost effortlessly, what your mother or best friend noticed about you. It's baked into your DNA, upbringing, and identity. It feels like play to you and looks like work to others. It's often technical or creative, cannot be outsourced or automated, and is learned through apprenticeships, not schools.

- **The 4 Kinds of Luck**: (1) Blind luck — completely out of your control. (2) Luck through persistence, hustle, and motion — generating energy until luck finds you. (3) Luck through preparedness — being so skilled in a field you spot lucky breaks others miss. (4) Luck through unique character/brand — your reputation causes luck to seek you out (the deep-sea diver example).
  - When to use: To get rich *without* getting lucky, engineer the 4th kind.
  - How: Become the best at what you do; refine what you do until this is true. Opportunity then seeks you out — luck becomes destiny.

- **The Leverage Trinity**: Business leverage comes from (1) labor — people working for you (oldest, messiest, worst form), (2) capital — money (dominant in the last century), and (3) code/media — products with no marginal cost of replication (newest, most democratic, where all new fortunes come from).
  - When to use: When choosing a career position or how to scale output.
  - How: Code and media are *permissionless* leverage — no one must give them to you. Capital and labor are *permissioned* — someone must hand them over, so they require credibility.

- **Long-Term Games with Long-Term People**: All returns in life — wealth, relationships, knowledge — come from compound interest. Play iterated games.
  - When to use: Choosing partners, employers, and deals.
  - How: Pick partners with high intelligence, energy, and above all integrity. Don't partner with cynics and pessimists — their beliefs are self-fulfilling. Nivi's formulation: in a long-term game everybody makes each other rich (positive sum); in a short-term game everybody makes themselves rich.

- **Accountability**: Take business risks under your own name. Society rewards you with responsibility, equity, and leverage.
  - How: Put your name on your output. It's a double-edged sword — credit when things go well, blame when they fail — but the downside risk in modern society is small; people forgive honest, high-integrity failures.

- **Set an Aspirational Hourly Rate**: Decide what your time is worth; it should feel absurdly high. If it doesn't feel absurd, raise it.
  - How: If fixing a problem saves less than your hourly rate, ignore it. If outsourcing costs less than your hourly rate, outsource it. (Naval used $5,000/hour before he had money.)

- **The 3 Big Decisions**: Where you live, who you're with, and what you do dominate your life trajectory.
  - How: Spend 1–2 years deciding these, not hours. If you're living in a city 10 years, in a job 5 years, in a relationship a decade, the decision deserves commensurate investment.

## Key Concepts
- **Equity**: Ownership of a business — the only route to financial freedom. Without it your inputs tie to your outputs and you can't earn nonlinearly.
- **Retirement**: When you stop sacrificing today for an imaginary tomorrow — when today is complete in and of itself. Reached via enough passive income, zero burn rate, or doing what you love.
- **Renting out your time**: The salaried trap; your employer pays you the bare minimum needed to keep you, and you never earn while sleeping.
- **Zero-sum status games**: Old hierarchical games where someone must lose. Avoid them; winning requires putting others down and makes you angry/combative.
- **Positive-sum money game**: Wealth creation — evolutionarily recent; you can get rich by giving society what it wants but doesn't know how to get, at scale.
- **Risk of ruin**: Avoid jail, physical danger, and losing all capital. Take rationally optimistic bets with big upsides instead.
- **Hedonic treadmill**: The endless upgrade of lifestyle as income rises; the lust for money is a bottomless pit.
- **Real résumé**: A catalog of all your suffering — the hard things you chose to do.

## Mental Models
- **Money is an IOU, wealth is the machine**: Think of wealth as the factory cranking out things while you sleep; money is just the transfer token. Pursue the factory.
- **Inputs vs outputs**: The higher the creativity component of a profession, the more disconnected inputs and outputs are. Optimize for jobs where output (judgment) matters, not hours.
- **Leveraged vs un-leveraged**: Forget rich vs poor — the real divide is whether your effort is multiplied 1,000x by leverage. A leveraged worker's judgment matters more than their hours.
- **Make money like a side effect**: Do things for their own sake (art); ironically you produce your best work and become most successful. Naval's richest year was the one he worked least hard.
- **Compound everything**: Reputation, relationships, and trust compound like capital. 99% of effort is wasted — find the 1% and go all-in on it.

## Anti-patterns
- **Grinding at hard work before knowing what to work on**: Work must be directed; figure out the target first.
- **Partnering with cynics and pessimists**: Their beliefs are self-fulfilling prophecies.
- **Playing status games**: Zero-sum; you must put others down, which corrupts you.
- **Chasing "business" degrees/magazines**: There is no skill called "business"; study microeconomics, game theory, psychology, persuasion, ethics, mathematics, and computers instead.
- **Get-rich-quick schemes**: "Those are just someone else getting rich off you."
- **Business networking**: Building relationships in advance of doing business is a waste. Be a maker; show your craft and the right people find you.
- **Counting time**: "If you're counting, you'll run out of patience before success actually arrives." Don't keep track of years put in.
- **Upgrading lifestyle as money comes in**: Hold lifestyle fixed and take income in lump sums to escape the treadmill.
- **Talking about how honest you are**: A tell — people who oversell their values are covering for something.

## Worked Example
**The real estate leverage ladder** — Naval's walk-through of five levels in one industry, showing escalating leverage + accountability + specific knowledge:
1. **Laborer repairing houses**: ~$10–20/hour. Zero leverage, no accountability, no specific knowledge → minimum wage.
2. **General contractor**: $50,000/project, pays labor $15/hour, keeps the difference. Some accountability, labor leverage, organizing skill.
3. **Real estate developer**: Buys property, raises money, tears down/rebuilds, sells — $500K–$1M profit. High accountability, capital leverage, fundraising and market knowledge.
4. **Real estate fund manager**: Enormous capital leverage; managing many developers and huge inventory.
5. **Tech + real estate company (Trulia/Redfin/Zillow)**: Specific knowledge in both domains, massive accountability, code leverage + capital + high-quality labor → upside in the billions.

The lesson: each level up adds leverage, accountability, and specific knowledge. Start as a salaried employee, work your way up that ladder, and combine with compound interest over decades.

## Key Takeaways
1. Wealth = owning assets that earn while you sleep; you won't get rich renting out your time — own equity.
2. Build specific knowledge (untrainable, playful, authentic), take accountability under your own name, and apply the highest permissionless leverage (code/media).
3. Play long-term games with long-term people; all returns compound, including reputation and trust.
4. Get paid for judgment, not hours — set an absurdly high hourly rate and ruthlessly outsource below it.
5. Spend real time (1–2 years) on the three big decisions: where you live, who you're with, what you do.
6. Engineer the 4th kind of luck: become the best at what you do, refine it until that's true, and opportunity seeks you out.
7. Be patient and don't count; karma is just you repeating patterns until you get what you deserve.
