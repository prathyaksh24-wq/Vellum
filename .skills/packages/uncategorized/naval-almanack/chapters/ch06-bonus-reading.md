# Chapter 6: Bonus — Recommended Reading, Writing & Formulas

## Core Idea
Naval's reading philosophy: read what you love until you love to read — not for self-improvement but out of curiosity — and let your taste evolve into theory, concepts, and nonfiction. His "Life Formulas" compress his entire system into equations.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The Reading Ladder**: "I started with comic books and sci-fi. Then I was into history and news. Then into psychology, popular science, technology."
  - When to use: When you don't know what to read.
  - How: Start where your interest is, even "junk" — "Read enough, and you become a connoisseur. Then you naturally gravitate more toward theory, concepts, nonfiction." The best book is the one you'll devour.

- **Naval's Life Formulas (2008)**: Algorithms for success, not definitions:
  - Happiness = Health + Wealth + Good Relationships
  - Health = Exercise + Diet + Sleep
  - Exercise = High Intensity Resistance Training + Sports + Rest
  - Diet = Natural Foods + Intermittent Fasting + Plants
  - Sleep = No alarms + 8–9 hours + Circadian rhythms
  - Wealth = Income + Wealth × ROI
  - Income = Accountability + Leverage + Specific Knowledge
  - Accountability = Personal Branding + Personal Platform + Taking Risk
  - Leverage = Capital + People + Intellectual Property
  - Specific Knowledge = Knowing how to do something society cannot yet easily train other people to do
  - ROI = Buy-and-Hold + Valuation + Margin of Safety
  - When to use: As a one-screen audit of your life; each term decomposes into the actionable components from Chapters 1–4.

- **Naval's Rules (2016)** — the complete maxim set: Be present above all else; Desire is suffering (Buddha); Anger is a hot coal you hold in your hand while waiting to throw it (Buddha); If you can't see yourself working with someone for life, don't work with them for a day; Reading (learning) is the ultimate meta-skill and can be traded for anything else; All the real benefits in life come from compound interest; Earn with your mind, not your time; 99 percent of all effort is wasted; Total honesty at all times (it's almost always possible to be honest and positive); Praise specifically, criticize generally (Buffett); Truth is that which has predictive power; Watch every thought ("Why am I having this thought?"); All greatness comes from suffering; Love is given, not received; Enlightenment is the space between your thoughts (Tolle); Mathematics is the language of nature; Every moment has to be complete in and of itself; **Health, love, and your mission, in that order. Nothing else matters.**

## Key Concepts
- **Curiosity over self-improvement**: "I don't read for self-improvement. I read out of curiosity and interest."
- **Books as investment**: "I always spent money on books. I never viewed that as an expense. That's an investment."
- **Read what you disagree with**: Naval skips two-thirds of his books when telling people what he reads — they're "embarrassing," trivial, or mind-bending. Read things you disagree with to bend your mind.
- **The connoisseur's library**: Reread the greats — "I'm pretty much always rereading something by either Krishnamurti or Osho."

## Key Categories of Recommended Reading
- **Nonfiction**: Sapiens (Harari — "best book of the last decade"), The Beginning of Infinity (Deutsch), The Rational Optimist and everything by Matt Ridley, Skin in the Game + all of Taleb (Incerto), Six Easy Pieces (Feynman), Thing Explainer (Munroe), Thinking Physics (Epstein), The Lessons of History (Durant), The Sovereign Individual, Poor Charlie's Almanack (Munger), Reality Is Not What It Seems + Seven Brief Lessons on Physics (Rovelli), The Compleat Strategyst (Williams), The Evolution of Cooperation (Axelrod).
- **Philosophy & Spirituality**: Everything by Jed McKenna ("spits raw truth"), everything by Kapil Gupta, The Book of Life + Total Freedom (Krishnamurti — "the 'spiritual' book I keep returning to"), Siddhartha (Hesse — most gifted book), The Book of Secrets (Osho — 112 meditations to try), Meditations (Marcus Aurelius — "absolutely life-changing"), The Untethered Soul (Singer), The Tao of Seneca (most important audiobook), How to Change Your Mind (Pollan — psychedelics as "a cheat code in self-observation"), Striking Thoughts (Bruce Lee), The Prophet (Gibran), Love Yourself Like Your Life Depends on It (Kamal Ravikant — the monk's "I say 'yes' to all that happens").
- **Science Fiction**: Ficciones/Labyrinths (Borges — "the most powerful author"), Stories of Your Life and Others + Exhalation + The Lifecycle of Software Objects (Ted Chiang), Snow Crash + The Diamond Age (Stephenson), "The Last Question" (Asimov), The Three-Body Problem (Cixin Liu).
- **Blogs & Essays**: Melting Asphalt (Simler — "Crony Beliefs"), Farnam Street (fs.blog — "The Munger Operating System"), Stratechery (Thompson — Aggregation Theory), Idle Words, "The Day You Became a Better Writer" (Scott Adams), "You and Your Research" (Hamming), "Career Decisions" (Elad Gil).

## Mental Models
- **Reading is the meta-skill**: "Reading (learning) is the ultimate meta-skill and can be traded for anything else."
- **The best workout analogy for books**: The best book to read is the one you're excited enough to read all the time — like the best workout is the one you'll do every day.
- **Intellectual compounding**: Like money, knowledge compounds — read the greats repeatedly.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reading for social approval**: "Almost everything that people read these days is designed for social approval" — regurgitated books about evolution instead of Darwin, treatises about economics instead of Adam Smith. Returns in life are out of the herd.
- **Reading what the news advocates**: "If you eat, invest, and think according to what the 'news' advocates, you'll end up nutritionally, financially, and morally bankrupt."
- **Skipping fiction/comics as "junk"**: Graphic novels (Transmetropolitan, The Boys, Planetary, The Sandman) are "among the finest works of art of our age."

## Key Takeaways
1. Read for curiosity, not self-improvement; junk builds the connoisseur.
2. Use the Life Formulas as a one-screen audit: happiness decomposes into health, wealth, and relationships — each into actionable variables.
3. Apply the Rules: health, love, and your mission, in that order; nothing else matters.
4. Start with originals and classics (Darwin, Adam Smith, Marcus Aurelius), not their regurgitators.
5. Spend money on books; reread the greats; read what you disagree with.
